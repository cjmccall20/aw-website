02The public site

Designed around the four things visitors actually come here to do — in priority order. Everything else supports those four paths.

Path 1

Sign up for public lessons

The biggest CTA on the homepage. The lessons page shows the current semester's classes with always-fresh dates and a one-click signup link. Officers schedule the whole semester in advance and flip sessions live when they're ready — no more stale 2/15–3/4 dates lingering for months.

Path 2

Get hyped for tryouts

Clear info on what tryouts look like, when they are, who can try out, and how prep classes work. A "tryouts open!" badge lights up the homepage during the active window and goes dark the rest of the year — automatically.

Path 3

Request a performance

A real intake form, not a tutorial video. Asks for venue (with Google address autocomplete), time, audience size, and a couple of judgment questions. The team gets an instant auto-reply and the request appears in the portal for the PR officer.

Path 4

Request a private lesson

For weddings, corporate events, and individuals who want one-on-one instruction. Same intake feel as the performance form, routes to the lessons officer.

Plus a real /watch destination

The team's video work — Midland's Burnout, Randy Rogers' I'll Never Get Over You, Ella Langley's Choosin' Texas, plus full live routines and behind-the-scenes content — gets a dedicated home. Three sections, all reorderable by the webmaster from inside the portal. Every video card has a "want to dance like this?" link straight to public lessons.

Every legacy page still works

Meet the Team, History, Our Building, FAQ, Alumni, Banquet, Merchandise, Sponsorships — all preserved at their existing URLs so the team's SEO equity transfers cleanly to the new site. Merchandise still links out to the existing store; banquet stays at /banquet year-round and gets updated annually.

04How a performance request flows

This is the marquee workflow — and the one that most needs to feel right. Read this as a single continuous story: from "someone fills out the form" to "the team shows up at the venue." Nothing leaves the portal as an email to the requester without an officer hitting Send.

Step 1
Request comes in
Form on the public site. Auto-reply sent ("we got it, expect a response within a few days").
Step 2
Auto-enrich
System computes drive time from the building to the venue and stores it on the request.
Step 3
Review
PR officer reads it. Decline, ask for info, or approve to poll the team.
Step 4
Weekly survey
Sunday 6 PM: members get one email with all open asks; respond yes/no/maybe per gig.
Step 5
Window closes
After 3 days the portal tallies responses and shows the PR officer who said yes.
Step 6
Officer decides
Looks at actual couples, conflicts, context. Clicks Confirm or Decline.
Step 7
Compose & send
In-portal composer opens prefilled. Officer edits, hits send. Calendar event auto-created with the right people on it.

The thing that changed: no autopilot on confirmation

Earlier versions of this plan had the system automatically draft a confirmation when enough couples said yes. We pulled back from that: the officer always makes the actual call. Conflicts with other gigs that night, requester history, last-minute changes — none of that is in the data, and a 5-of-8 yes vote isn't always a "go." So the portal surfaces the responses and waits for the officer.

What members see

Each item in the weekly survey shows the real time commitment based on the computed drive time:

"Wedding · Sat May 23 · Brenham, TX · 1h 15m drive — you'd need to be available roughly 4:45 PM to 10:45 PM."

No more guessing whether a 7 PM gig in Brenham is doable. The system already did the math.

The defaults (all changeable)

3 couples
minimum yes-votes before considering a request
3 days
to respond to a weekly survey
Sun 6 PM
default survey send time (CT)
30 days
default polling window before a gig

PR officer can adjust any of these globally, or per individual request if a particular booking needs a longer/shorter window. The whole weekly survey can be disabled entirely during breaks or finals.

05Institutional memory (the Contacts tab)

This might be the single feature that pays the most dividends over time — and it's invisible until you need it. Every person the team has ever interacted with becomes a contact. Every email, every request, every officer's notes about them — all of it stays attached to that contact across officer transitions, forever.

Today (no system)

A Houston wedding planner emails the team in 2027 asking about availability. The current PR officer doesn't recognize the name. She might be a one-time requester, or she might be the planner who booked the team three times between 2022 and 2024 and was specific about the call-time setup. The current officer has no way to know — that knowledge graduated three years ago.

With the new portal

The same email comes in. The PR officer types the planner's name into Contacts. Up comes:

"Booked us for the Stevens-Hill wedding (June 2022), the Reyes corporate gala (Sept 2023), and the Patel reception (May 2024). Always wants 90 minutes of call time. Pays via wire from corporate account. Florist is Stems by Sarah. Said our Midland video is what convinced their boss. Reply quickly — she's been a great client."

That note was written by the 2023 PR officer. The new officer reads it in five seconds and responds like the team has been working with this planner forever — because, institutionally, they have.

The same logic applies to recurring corporate clients, frequent private-lesson requesters, sponsors, alumni who keep coming back to volunteer, and anyone else worth remembering. Notes survive every officer transition.

06Calendar & staying in the loop

The portal is the source of truth for every team event — confirmed performances, scheduled lessons, tryouts, rehearsals. Three ways to see what's coming.

In the portal

Native calendar

Month, week, or agenda view inside the portal. Filter to just your events. Color-coded by event type. RSVP inline.

On your phone

Personal iCal feed

One link. Subscribe once in Apple, Google, or Outlook calendar — every event you're assigned to shows up there automatically and stays in sync. Forever.

In your inbox

Monday digest

Every Monday at 8 AM you get one email: "here's where you're expected this week." Wedding Saturday, CW1 class Wednesday, call times included.

Coming later (not v1)

Two-way sync with Outlook calendar and Google Calendar (so confirmed performances show up in your work calendar without an iCal subscription, and you can RSVP from your phone calendar app). Layered on after launch if there's real demand.

07How email works

Right now: confirmation emails, declines, follow-ups all live in whoever happened to be the PR officer's personal Gmail. When they graduate, the institutional record graduates with them. The new portal fixes this by making the portal itself the email client for client-facing communication.

Today

Officer reads a request on the public site, opens Outlook, writes a confirmation from their personal officer email. The email lives in their inbox. Next officer takes over — that thread is gone.

With the new portal

Officer clicks Compose Confirmation from inside the request. A template prefills with the right details (date, venue, call time). Officer edits and hits Send. The system sends from performance@, BCCs the team Outlook mailbox, and stores the whole exchange against the contact — visible to every future officer.

Two kinds of email, two paths

Auto-sent

The low-stakes stuff

Form submission acknowledgments, magic-link sign-in emails, weekly survey invitations, Monday digest. All templated, all sent automatically — nothing in them needs review.

Officer-composed

The client-facing stuff

Confirmations, declines, quotes, follow-ups. Always reviewed and sent by an officer from the in-portal composer. Templates prefill the boilerplate; the officer's edits are the personalization.

09What's in v1 vs. coming later

A few features that would be great to have eventually are intentionally not in the launch — to keep the build focused and the launch date credible. The portal is designed so any of these can layer on later without disrupting what's already running.

In the launch

Public site
All four CTAs, /watch, every legacy page
Performance pipeline
End-to-end with drive time + manual confirm
Lesson scheduling
Public sessions + private lessons
Contacts + email composer
The CRM and email-thread store
In-portal calendar + iCal
Source of truth in the portal; subscribe from anywhere
Move Library catalog
Metadata + links to private YouTube videos

Saved for after launch

  • SMS via TwilioDay-of reminder texts, "RSVP last call." Phone numbers are collected from day one so this can light up without re-asking everyone.
  • Outlook + Google Calendar two-way syncFor now you subscribe via iCal. Later, confirmed events can write directly into officers' work calendars.
  • Stripe paymentsPublic lessons stay on Flywire for now. Stripe gets integrated at the next natural pricing change.
  • Embedded merch galleryCurrently the merch page just links to the existing external store.
  • Move Library: video uploads + alumni contributionsIf managing access via YouTube channel membership ever stops being good enough, we layer on a contribution queue and per-move visibility controls.
  • Brand refreshThe launch ships on a placeholder palette and typography. The full brand work (logo, name, palette, voice) is a separate workstream — when it lands, it's a config change, not a rebuild.

10Where we need your input

Most architectural decisions are locked in. What's still open is operational stuff that needs real-world answers from people who know the team. This is the most useful place for non-technical feedback.

1Who is the system's point person on launch day, and who inherits it?

Someone on the current officer slate needs to own admin access. We need a successor plan for when they graduate.

2What's the team's primary communication channel — GroupMe, iMessage, Discord?

Not architectural, but affects the wording of the Monday digest and alert nudges. ("Hey, check the survey in GroupMe" vs. "in the Discord.")

3Which emails should officers send from?

Confirmations come from performance@? bookings@? president@? Affects how clients perceive replies. Also: do we authenticate sending on wranglers.tamu.edu (likely needs TAMU IT involvement) or on a team-owned domain we control directly?

4Bootstrap officer whitelist

We need the email addresses of the current officer slate so they can sign in immediately on launch day without going through an approval queue.

5Inbound mail setup

Each role mailbox needs a forwarding rule into the portal so replies from clients automatically attach to the right thread. Is there a team Office 365 admin who can set those up?

6Flywire signup links — same URL every semester or new one each cycle?

Determines whether the lessons officer pastes a new URL each semester or just flips an existing one back to active.

7YouTube channel for Move Library — who manages access?

Move videos live on the team's private YouTube channel. Alumni get access how — invitation through YouTube directly, or via a managed Google Group? Affects what we tell alumni about how to watch.

8Photo/video rights audit

Anything currently on the public Wix site (or in legacy archives) we shouldn't republish? Worth a quick review before we migrate the images over.

9Brand refresh timing

Recommended path: launch on the placeholder theme and swap in the brand refresh after. But: is there appetite to delay launch until the brand work is done? Tradeoff is "live faster but rebrand later" vs. "wait but launch with the final look."

Feedback is welcome — and useful.

If anything here doesn't match how the team actually works, or you've spotted a gap in the workflow, or there's a question you can answer from operational experience — we want to hear it before the build starts. Faster to change the plan than to change the system.
