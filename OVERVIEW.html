A new website + a back-office that runs itself.

We're replacing the current Wix site with two things that work together: a polished public site that gets people to sign up, request, and watch — and a team portal that handles the actual operational life of the Wranglers (performance requests, scheduling, member roster, institutional memory).

02The public site

Designed around the four things visitors actually come here to do — in priority order. Everything else supports those four paths.

Path 1

Sign up for public lessons

The biggest CTA on the homepage. The lessons page shows the current semester's classes with always-fresh dates and a one-click signup link. Officers schedule the whole semester in advance and flip sessions live when they're ready — no more stale 2/15–3/4 dates lingering for months.

Path 2

Get hyped for tryouts

Clear info on what tryouts look like, when they are, who can try out, and how prep classes work. A "tryouts open!" badge lights up the homepage during the active window and goes dark the rest of the year — automatically.

Path 3

Request a performance

A real intake form, not a tutorial video. Asks for venue (with Google address autocomplete), time, audience size, and a couple of judgment questions. The team gets an instant auto-reply and the request appears in the portal for the PR officer.

Path 4

Request a private lesson

For weddings, corporate events, and individuals who want one-on-one instruction. Same intake feel as the performance form, routes to the lessons officer.

Plus: "notify me when new lessons drop"

Visitors who land on the lessons page when sessions are full, dates don't work, or the next round isn't scheduled yet can drop their email in a "get notified" widget. Same goes for tryouts. When the lessons coordinator schedules a new semester, they click "Notify the list" in the portal — the system pre-drafts an announcement email, the coordinator reviews and sends, and the whole subscriber list gets the news from lessons@aggiewranglers.com with a one-click unsubscribe link.

Plus a real /watch destination

The team's video work — Midland's Burnout, Randy Rogers' I'll Never Get Over You, Ella Langley's Choosin' Texas, plus full live routines and behind-the-scenes content — gets a dedicated home. Three sections, all reorderable by the webmaster from inside the portal. Every video card has a "want to dance like this?" link straight to public lessons.

Every legacy page still works

Meet the Team, History, Our Building, FAQ, Alumni, Banquet, Merchandise, Sponsorships — all preserved at their existing URLs so the team's SEO equity transfers cleanly to the new site. Merchandise still links out to the existing store; banquet stays at /banquet year-round and gets updated annually.

03The team portal — 15 areas

Officers create new member profiles in the portal; new members get an invite-link email to set their password (or just use magic-link sign-in going forward). What you can see and do depends on your permission status(es) — and the matrix is fully configurable in Settings, not hardcoded. The team can reshape access over time as roles evolve. Below: what each area actually does.

1Dashboard

Your week at a glance: where you're expected, surveys you owe a response to, action items keyed to your role, annual-reminder pings.

2Performance Management

Every performance request, from new lead to confirmed booking. The performance officer's command center.

3Lessons Management

Schedule a whole semester of public sessions, plan the private-lesson queue, manage the instructor pool, send announcements to the "notify me when new lessons drop" subscriber list.

4Contacts (CRM)

Every person AND organization the team has talked to — with full request, email, and notes history. Recurring sorority booking the team every year? That's an org-contact with rotating contact people. Institutional memory lives here.

5Members

Roster CRUD: name, photo, partner, role, status. TAMU + personal email both stored; system flips primary to personal on graduation. Officers create new profiles; system sends an invite link.

6Webmaster

The CMS for the public site. Edit homepage wording, profiles, FAQ, sponsors, video listings, banquet info — without touching code. Not a title; anyone with permission can use it.

7Team Calendar

The team's existing Google Calendar with 2-way sync. Add events here, edit in Google Calendar, either way both stay in sync. Recurring practices and officer meetings supported.

8Resources

Constitution, contracts, choreography notes, sponsor decks — uploaded once, available to whoever should see them.

9Move Library

A catalog of every jitt and stunt move the team knows, with links to the team's private YouTube videos.

10Alumni Directory

Searchable by grad year and current city. Alumni manage their own profile and contact preferences.

11Meeting Notes

Secretary tab: date, attendees, agenda, decisions, action items with assignees and due dates. Searchable across past meetings.

12Long-Term Goals

President-maintained doc for multi-year priorities. Inherits forward at officer transition so strategic thinking carries between presidents.

13Performance Stats

Per-member attendance %, per-performance roster, individual performance history. Drop-out rate, response rate, team-wide trends.

14Surveys

The combined performance + private-lesson availability survey, member-facing. Past responses visible.

15Settings

The permissions matrix (who-can-see-what), email and calendar config, system defaults. Mostly president + admin.

04How a performance request flows

This is the marquee workflow — and the one that most needs to feel right. Read this as a single continuous story: from "someone fills out the form" to "the team shows up at the venue." Nothing leaves the portal as an email to the requester without an officer hitting Send.

Step 1
Request comes in
Form on the public site. Auto-reply sent ("we got it, expect a response within a few days").
Step 2
Auto-enrich
System computes drive time from the building to the venue and stores it on the request.
Step 3
Review
PR officer reads it. Decline, ask for info, or approve to poll the team.
Step 4
Wednesday survey
Wednesday 6 PM: members get ONE email with all open performance + private-lesson asks; respond yes/no/maybe per gig.
Step 5
Window closes
After 3 days the portal tallies responses and shows the performance officer who said yes.
Step 6
Officer decides
Looks at actual couples, conflicts, context. Clicks Confirm or Decline.
Step 7
Send from Outlook
Portal opens an Outlook draft prefilled with confirmation details. Officer reviews, edits, sends from Outlook. Google Calendar event auto-created with the right people on it.

No autopilot on confirmation, ever

Earlier versions had the system auto-draft a confirmation when enough couples said yes. We pulled back from that: the officer always makes the actual call. Conflicts with other gigs that night, requester history, last-minute changes — none of that is in the data, and a 5-of-8 yes vote isn't always a "go." The portal surfaces the responses and waits for the officer.

Officers send from Outlook, not from the portal

In an earlier draft the portal had its own email composer. We removed it. The team already lives in their TAMU Outlook (`performance@wranglers.tamu.edu`, etc.) — so the portal opens a prefilled draft in Outlook via a `mailto:` link instead. Officer reviews and sends from where they already work. A behind-the-scenes BCC rule on each officer's Outlook copies sent mail into the portal's CRM history (see Email section).

What members see

Each item in the Wednesday survey shows the real time commitment based on the computed drive time:

"Wedding · Sat May 23 · Brenham, TX · 1h 15m drive — you'd need to be available roughly 4:45 PM to 10:45 PM."

No more guessing whether a 7 PM gig in Brenham is doable. The system already did the math.

The defaults (all changeable)

3 couples
minimum yes-votes before considering a request
3 days
to respond to a weekly survey
Wed 6 PM
default survey send time (CT)
30 days
default polling window before a gig

Performance officer can adjust any of these globally, or per individual request if a particular booking needs a longer/shorter window. The whole weekly survey can be disabled entirely during breaks or finals.

Why Wednesday: sends Wed 6 PM, responses close Saturday, officer decides over the weekend, weekly digest goes out Monday morning with confirmed assignments for the week.

Performance request form also asks about donations ($250 / $500 / $750 / $1,000 / Other / Not at this time). The portal captures interest only — the team handles actual donation collection outside the portal. After the gig, the officer can mark "Received / Declined / No response / Pending" for institutional memory.

Confirmed performance rosters stay editable. If someone drops out or someone else needs to step in, one click; the affected members get notified.

05Institutional memory (the Contacts tab)

This might be the single feature that pays the most dividends over time — and it's invisible until you need it. Every person the team has ever interacted with becomes a contact. Every email, every request, every officer's notes about them — all of it stays attached to that contact across officer transitions, forever.

Today (no system)

A Houston wedding planner emails the team in 2027 asking about availability. The current PR officer doesn't recognize the name. She might be a one-time requester, or she might be the planner who booked the team three times between 2022 and 2024 and was specific about the call-time setup. The current officer has no way to know — that knowledge graduated three years ago.

With the new portal

The same email comes in. The PR officer types the planner's name into Contacts. Up comes:

"Booked us for the Stevens-Hill wedding (June 2022), the Reyes corporate gala (Sept 2023), and the Patel reception (May 2024). Always wants 90 minutes of call time. Pays via wire from corporate account. Florist is Stems by Sarah. Said our Midland video is what convinced their boss. Reply quickly — she's been a great client."

That note was written by the 2023 PR officer. The new officer reads it in five seconds and responds like the team has been working with this planner forever — because, institutionally, they have.

The same logic applies to recurring corporate clients, frequent private-lesson requesters, sponsors, alumni who keep coming back to volunteer, and anyone else worth remembering. Notes survive every officer transition.

06Calendar & staying in the loop

The team's existing Google Calendar is the source of truth — the portal syncs with it 2-way. Add a confirmed performance in the portal? It appears on the team Google Calendar. Edit an event directly in Google Calendar? The portal picks up the change. Same calendar carries forward at officer handover.

In Google Calendar

The team calendar

The calendar the team already uses. Confirmed performances, scheduled lessons, tryouts, recurring practices, officer meetings — all there. Members subscribe directly from their phone.

In the portal

Calendar UI

A clean month/week/day view inside the portal that reads from and writes to Google Calendar. Filter to just your events. Add events; they sync. Edit events; they sync. RSVP inline.

In your inbox

Monday digest

Every Monday at 8 AM you get one email: "here's where you're expected this week." Wedding Saturday, CW1 class Wednesday, call times included.

Why Google Calendar instead of an in-portal calendar

Earlier drafts had the portal own its own calendar with one-way iCal export to phones. The team feedback was clear: everyone already uses Google Calendar. Adding a second calendar to keep in sync manually would be a worse experience than just integrating with the one they already trust. Calendar ownership transfers cleanly between presidents — same calendar, new admin.

07How email works

Officers keep writing emails in their existing TAMU Outlook (where they already work). The portal observes — capturing every sent message into the right contact's history so it survives officer turnover.

Today

Officer reads a request, opens Outlook, writes a confirmation from performance@wranglers.tamu.edu. The thread lives in their inbox. Next officer takes over the role mailbox — they can scroll back through Outlook, but realistically nobody digs through years of old emails to find the one client.

With the new portal

Officer clicks "Compose Confirmation" in the portal. An Outlook draft opens with the right details prefilled. Officer edits and hits Send from Outlook. A behind-the-scenes Outlook rule BCCs the sent message to the portal, which attaches it to the contact's profile. Years later, the next officer opens that contact and sees the whole conversation.

Two kinds of email, two paths

Auto-sent

The low-stakes stuff

Form submission acknowledgments, magic-link sign-in emails, Wednesday survey invitations, Monday digest. All templated, all sent automatically from wranglers@aggiewranglers.com (or the relevant role alias). Nothing in them needs human review.

Officer-composed

The client-facing stuff

Confirmations, declines, quotes, follow-ups. Always written and sent by an officer from their TAMU Outlook. Portal generates a prefilled draft via a "Compose…" button; officer reviews, edits, sends. No in-portal composer to maintain or learn.

Why no portal composer?

Earlier drafts had the portal own its own composer. Two problems: (1) it meant authenticating the team's sending domain with A&M IT, which is harder than it sounds; (2) officers would be learning a new email tool instead of using the Outlook they already know. The v6 architecture uses the team's existing Outlook for composing and only the portal for capture. Everyone keeps working where they already work.

Permissions: configurable, not hardcoded

A subtle but important architectural choice: who can see and edit what is not hardcoded. The Settings tab has a grid editor where the team decides — for each role (status) and each tab — whether that role has none / view / edit access. Multiple users can hold the same role (so during officer turnover, both the outgoing and incoming Performance Officer can have access for a transition month). Defaults ship with sensible access; the team reconfigures over time as the structure evolves.

08How we'll build it

Focused build, then a single launch day — the new public site and the portal go live together. No incremental rollout; people see the new world once it's ready.

0Phase

Foundation

Set up the project, database, sign-in, email sending domain on Cloudflare + Resend, Google Calendar API access. Pre-create current officer profiles so they can sign in day one.

1Phase

Public site shell + design system

Every legacy page rebuilt. Photos migrated from Wix. Mobile-first, fast. /our-building redirects to home.

2Phase

Portal foundation: Permissions + Members + Contacts

Sign-in via magic-link / invite-link works. The permission matrix is editable in Settings. Roster CRUD. Contacts tab — people + organizations, auto-create on form submit with matching logic.

3Phase

Public forms + auto-replies

The four forms (including the new donation interest field on performance requests) are live, write to the portal, and trigger auto-replies attached to the right contact.

4Phase

Email archive + Webmaster + Performance Mgmt + Wednesday survey + drive-time

The biggest phase. BCC archive ingest from officer Outlooks. Webmaster CMS for the public site. Full performance lifecycle. Drive-time computation. Wednesday combined availability survey. Manual confirm/decline via Outlook drafts.

5Phase

Lessons management

Semester scheduling, private-lesson workflow merged into the Wednesday survey, instructor pool.

6Phase

Google Calendar 2-way sync + Monday digest

Portal reads and writes to the team's existing Google Calendar. Recurring practices, officer meetings, retreats supported. Monday morning "where you're expected" digest email.

7Phase

Resources, Move Library, Alumni, Meeting Notes, Long-Term Goals

File library, move catalog with YouTube links, alumni directory + self-signup, secretary meeting notes tab, president long-term goals tab.

8Phase

Performance Stats + /watch + homepage polish

Attendance %, per-performance roster view, individual history. The video destination plus final homepage tuning.

9Phase

SEO migration + launch

Stage everything, verify Search Console, flip DNS — go live.

10Phase

Officer handoff

Loom walkthroughs per role, printable cheat sheets, the one-time Outlook auto-BCC setup per officer, transition docs.

09What's in v1 vs. coming later

A few features that would be great to have eventually are intentionally not in the launch — to keep the build focused and the launch date credible. The portal is designed so any of these can layer on later without disrupting what's already running.

In the launch

Public site
All four CTAs, /watch, every legacy page; donation field on performance form
Performance pipeline
End-to-end with drive time + manual confirm + editable rosters
Lesson scheduling
Public sessions + private lessons; merged into one Wednesday survey
Contacts (CRM)
People + organizations; full history fed by Outlook BCC archive
Google Calendar 2-way sync
Existing team calendar; recurring practices supported
Webmaster CMS
Edit homepage / profiles / FAQ / sponsors without code
Configurable permissions
Settings matrix; reconfigurable as team structure changes
Stats dashboard
Attendance %, per-performance rosters, individual history
Move Library catalog
Metadata + links to private YouTube videos
"Notify me" lists
For new lesson sessions + tryout cycles; one-click coordinator send

Saved for after launch

  • Social media management tabCross-platform posting (IG, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, X), maybe with AI-generated per-platform variants. Team can use Buffer or similar in the interim.
  • Online courses + Coaching serviceCountry-western fundamentals + wedding first-dance prep + personalized video reviews. Sales routed entirely through TAMU's SOFC Marketplace; courses host on Teachable-class platforms with code-required enrollment.
  • SMS via TwilioDay-of reminder texts, "RSVP last call." Phone numbers are collected from day one so this lights up without re-asking everyone.
  • Embedded merch galleryCurrently the merch page just links to the existing external store.
  • Move Library: video uploads + alumni contributionsIf managing access via YouTube channel membership ever stops being good enough, we layer on a contribution queue and per-move visibility controls.
  • Brand refreshThe launch ships on a placeholder palette and typography. The full brand work (logo, name, palette, voice) is a separate workstream — when it lands, it's a config change, not a rebuild.
Donations: explicitly NOT processed by the portal

The performance request form captures donation interest at intake; the officer can mark received/declined/no-response after the event. Actual collection happens through whatever channel the current team uses (cash/check at the event, etc.) — SOFC doesn't process donations and the portal doesn't either.

10Where we need your input

Most architectural decisions are locked in. What's still open is operational stuff that needs real-world answers from people who know the team. This is the most useful place for non-technical feedback.

1Bootstrap officer whitelist

We need the email addresses of the current officer slate so we can pre-create their profiles and they get setup invites on launch day.

2What's the team's primary communication channel — GroupMe, iMessage, Discord?

Not architectural, but affects the wording of the Monday digest and staleness-alert nudges.

3Outlook auto-BCC rules on TAMU email — known to work?

The CRM history depends on each officer setting up a one-time "always BCC archive@aggiewranglers.com on sent mail" rule in their TAMU Outlook. Most Exchange tenants allow this; some restrict it. If TAMU's setup blocks it, we fall back to forwarding rules (slightly noisier but works). Worth checking once we have access.

4Flywire signup links for public lessons — same URL every semester or new one each cycle?

Determines whether the lessons coordinator pastes a new URL each semester or just flips an existing one back to active.

5YouTube channel for Move Library — who manages access?

Move videos live on the team's private YouTube channel. Alumni get access how — invitation through YouTube directly, or via a managed Google Group? Affects what we tell alumni about how to watch.

6Photo/video rights audit

Anything currently on the public Wix site (or in legacy archives) we shouldn't republish? Worth a quick review before we migrate the images over.

7Brand refresh timing

Recommended path: launch on the placeholder theme and swap in the brand refresh after. But: is there appetite to delay launch until the brand work is done?

8Donations — how does the team collect today?

Portal captures donation interest and post-event status only; actual collection happens outside the portal. Cash/check at the event? Something else? Affects the wording of the post-performance prompt to the officer.

Feedback is welcome — and useful.

If anything here doesn't match how the team actually works, or you've spotted a gap in the workflow, or there's a question you can answer from operational experience — we want to hear it before the build starts. Faster to change the plan than to change the system.
